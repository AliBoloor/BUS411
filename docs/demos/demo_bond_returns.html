The desk question

A client buys a bond yielding 5%. Will the client actually earn 5% over the next two years? Build a horizon-value report that separates coupon income, reinvestment income, and the change in the bond’s full price. Then explain which assumptions make a quoted yield an attainable compound return.

Purpose and assumptions

Use an illustrative five-year, 6% coupon bond with face value $100. Coupons are semiannual. Buy immediately after a coupon at a 5% nominal annual YTM and sell immediately after the year-2 coupon. All cash flows are contractual; ignore default, calls, taxes, fees, and bid–ask spreads. Accrued interest is zero at both endpoints. Exit YTM and coupon reinvestment rates are independent scenario inputs, quoted as nominal annual rates compounded semiannually. No inputs are live quotes.

Read alongside Lecture 2. Dollar results below are per $100 face unless explicitly scaled to the client position.

1 Price the promised cash flows

With \(n=10\) half-year periods and \(C=100(0.06)/2=3\),

\[ P_0=\sum_{k=1}^{10}\frac{3}{(1+0.05/2)^k} +\frac{100}{(1+0.05/2)^{10}}. \]

def bond_price(coupon_rate, periods, annual_yield, face=100.):
    """Ex-coupon full price on a semiannual coupon date; rates are decimals.

    `periods` is the number of remaining half years. A zero-period bond has
    just matured and returns principal; coupon accounting is handled separately.
    Yield can be scalar or an array. Payment dates occupy the final axis.
    """
    y = np.asarray(annual_yield, dtype=float)
    if not np.all(np.isfinite(y)) or np.any(y <= -2):
        raise ValueError("Semiannual yields must be finite and greater than -200%.")
    if int(periods) != periods or periods < 0 or not 0 <= coupon_rate < 1 or face <= 0:
        raise ValueError("Use nonnegative integer periods, a valid coupon, and positive face.")
    if periods == 0:
        return np.full_like(y, face, dtype=float)
    k = np.arange(1, periods + 1)
    cf = np.full(periods, face*coupon_rate/2)
    cf[-1] += face
    return np.sum(cf*np.exp(-np.log1p(y[...,None]/2)*k), axis=-1)

2 Distinguish quoted yields from realized returns

Current yield is annual coupon divided by today’s price. It ignores both reinvestment and the gain or loss when the bond is sold or redeemed. YTM is the internal rate of return of promised payments at the purchase price. It is not a forecast of a short-horizon sale price.

A 5% semiannual-compounding YTM corresponds to an effective annual rate of \((1.025)^2-1=5.0625\%\). Realizing that compound rate requires the relevant reinvestment and terminal-value assumptions, as the next calculation shows.

3 Build terminal wealth without double counting coupons

At \(H=2\) years, four coupons have been received. The sale price is ex-coupon, with six half-year payments remaining. If \(j\) is the annual reinvestment rate,

\[ FV_C=3\left[(1+j/2)^3+(1+j/2)^2+(1+j/2)+1\right], \]

\[ W_H=P_H+FV_C,\qquad HPR=\frac{W_H-P_0}{P_0},\qquad R_{\mathrm{annual}}=\left(\frac{W_H}{P_0}\right)^{1/H}-1. \]

The coupon paid at year 2 enters \(FV_C\) once and earns no reinvestment interest. The sale price excludes that coupon. This is a terminal-wealth return that assumes all coupon cash remains invested until the horizon.

def horizon_report(exit_yield, reinvest_rate, horizon_periods=4):
    """Return a scenario table of terminal wealth and realized return.

    Rates are decimal nominal annual rates; horizon is an integer number of
    half years in [1, 10]. Scalars and arrays broadcast to a common shape.
    Coupons are reinvested at a constant scenario rate, without withdrawals.
    """
    if int(horizon_periods) != horizon_periods or not 1 <= horizon_periods <= maturity_periods:
        raise ValueError("Horizon must be a coupon date through maturity.")
    ey, ri = np.broadcast_arrays(np.atleast_1d(exit_yield), np.atleast_1d(reinvest_rate))
    if not np.all(np.isfinite(ri)) or np.any(ri <= -2):
        raise ValueError("Reinvestment rates must be finite and greater than -200%.")
    coupon_cash = 3*horizon_periods
    powers = np.arange(horizon_periods-1, -1, -1)
    coupon_fv = 3*np.exp(np.log1p(ri[...,None]/2)*powers).sum(axis=-1)
    sale = bond_price(coupon_rate, maturity_periods-horizon_periods, ey)
    wealth = sale+coupon_fv
    return pd.DataFrame({"Exit YTM (%)": 100*ey.ravel(), "Reinvest (%)": 100*ri.ravel(),
        "Sale / principal": sale.ravel(), "Coupon cash": np.full(ey.size,coupon_cash),
        "Reinvestment income": (coupon_fv-coupon_cash).ravel(),
        "Price change": (sale-purchase_price).ravel(), "Terminal wealth": wealth.ravel(),
        "HPR (%)": 100*(wealth/purchase_price-1).ravel(),
        "Annual return (%)": 100*np.expm1(np.log(wealth/purchase_price)/(horizon_periods/2)).ravel()})

Interpretation and decision. The coupon rate determines contractual cash payments; it does not measure profit relative to the premium purchase price. The purchase YTM describes promised cash-flow discounting, while the horizon return also depends on sale price and reinvestment. The 10% exit scenario shows that coupon income need not prevent a loss. This five-year bond does not assure a positive two-year return. A default-free zero-coupon claim maturing at the client’s horizon better matches a fixed nominal liability and eliminates coupon reinvestment risk. Its purchase price still determines its return; inflation, transaction costs, and selling before maturity remain relevant.

def readable_table(frame):
    """Format a teaching table without changing its underlying numerical values.

    Parameters
    ----------
    frame : pandas.DataFrame
        Display data. attrs["whole_table"] requests a single reconciliation
        table; attrs["decimals"] chooses the number of displayed decimals.

    Returns
    -------
    str
        HTML markup suitable for both Quarto HTML and PDF conversion.

    Notes
    -----
    Wide, short tables are transposed. Other wide tables are split into panels.
    Formatting applies only at display time, preserving full-precision results.
    """
    whole_table = frame.attrs.get("whole_table", False)
    decimals = frame.attrs.get("decimals", 4)
    frame = frame.copy()
    if not whole_table and len(frame.columns) and frame.iloc[:, 0].dtype == object:
        frame = frame.set_index(frame.columns[0])
    if not whole_table and len(frame.columns) > 6 and len(frame) <= 4:
        frame = frame.T
    panels = []
    panel_width = len(frame.columns) if whole_table else 3
    for start in range(0, len(frame.columns), panel_width):
        part = frame.iloc[:, start:start+panel_width]
        markup = part.to_html(border=0, na_rep="", float_format=lambda v: f"{v:,.{decimals}f}")
        widths = [40] + [60 / max(1, len(part.columns))] * len(part.columns)
        cols = "<colgroup>" + "".join(f'<col style="width:{w}%">' for w in widths) + "</colgroup>"
        end = markup.index(">") + 1
        panels.append(markup[:end] + cols + markup[end:])
    return "\n".join(panels)

## Price the promised cash flows

With $n=10$ half-year periods and $C=100(0.06)/2=3$,

$$
P_0=\sum_{k=1}^{10}\frac{3}{(1+0.05/2)^k}
       +\frac{100}{(1+0.05/2)^{10}}.
$$

```{python}
#| label: returns-setup
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from IPython.display import Markdown, display

plt.rcParams.update({"figure.figsize": (8, 4.2), "font.size": 11,
    "axes.spines.top": False, "axes.spines.right": False,
    "axes.grid": True, "grid.alpha": .2})

def bond_price(coupon_rate, periods, annual_yield, face=100.):
    """Ex-coupon full price on a semiannual coupon date; rates are decimals.

    `periods` is the number of remaining half years. A zero-period bond has
    just matured and returns principal; coupon accounting is handled separately.
    Yield can be scalar or an array. Payment dates occupy the final axis.
    """
    y = np.asarray(annual_yield, dtype=float)
    if not np.all(np.isfinite(y)) or np.any(y <= -2):
        raise ValueError("Semiannual yields must be finite and greater than -200%.")
    if int(periods) != periods or periods < 0 or not 0 <= coupon_rate < 1 or face <= 0:
        raise ValueError("Use nonnegative integer periods, a valid coupon, and positive face.")
    if periods == 0:
        return np.full_like(y, face, dtype=float)
    k = np.arange(1, periods + 1)
    cf = np.full(periods, face*coupon_rate/2)
    cf[-1] += face
    return np.sum(cf*np.exp(-np.log1p(y[...,None]/2)*k), axis=-1)

def show_table(frame, decimals=4):
    """Keep short reports together in PDF and retain clear column labels."""
    if frame.shape[1] > 4:
        frame = frame.set_index(frame.columns[0]).T.reset_index().rename(columns={"index":"Measure"})
    reserve = "```{=latex}\n\\ReserveTableSpace{" + str(len(frame)+4) + "\\baselineskip}\n```\n\n"
    def cell(value):
        if isinstance(value, (int, float, np.number)):
            return f"{value:.{decimals}f}"
        return str(value).replace("|", "\\|")
    rows = ["| " + " | ".join(map(str, frame.columns)) + " |",
            "| " + " | ".join(["---"]*len(frame.columns)) + " |"]
    rows += ["| " + " | ".join(cell(v) for v in row) + " |"
             for row in frame.itertuples(index=False, name=None)]
    display(Markdown(reserve+"\n".join(rows)))

coupon_rate, maturity_periods, purchase_yield = .06, 10, .05
purchase_price = float(bond_price(coupon_rate, maturity_periods, purchase_yield))
periods = np.arange(1, 11)
cf = np.full(10, 3.); cf[-1] += 100
payments = pd.DataFrame({"Year": periods/2, "Payment": cf,
    "Discount factor": (1+purchase_yield/2)**(-periods),
    "Present value": cf/(1+purchase_yield/2)**periods})
show_table(payments,5)
display(Markdown(f"**Purchase full price: {purchase_price:.6f}.** The 6% coupon exceeds the 5% YTM, so the bond trades above par."))
```

## Distinguish quoted yields from realized returns

Current yield is annual coupon divided by today's price. It ignores both
reinvestment and the gain or loss when the bond is sold or redeemed. YTM is the
internal rate of return of promised payments at the purchase price. It is not a
forecast of a short-horizon sale price.

```{python}
#| label: return-quote-comparison
display(pd.DataFrame({"Measure": ["Coupon rate", "Current yield", "Nominal annual YTM", "Effective annual YTM"],
    "Percent": [6., 100*6/purchase_price, 5., 100*((1+.05/2)**2-1)]}).round(4))
```

A 5% semiannual-compounding YTM corresponds to an effective annual rate of
$(1.025)^2-1=5.0625\%$. Realizing that compound rate requires the relevant
reinvestment and terminal-value assumptions, as the next calculation shows.

## Build terminal wealth without double counting coupons

At $H=2$ years, four coupons have been received. The sale price is ex-coupon,
with six half-year payments remaining. If $j$ is the annual reinvestment rate,

$$
FV_C=3\left[(1+j/2)^3+(1+j/2)^2+(1+j/2)+1\right],
$$

$$
W_H=P_H+FV_C,\qquad
HPR=\frac{W_H-P_0}{P_0},\qquad
R_{\mathrm{annual}}=\left(\frac{W_H}{P_0}\right)^{1/H}-1.
$$

The coupon paid at year 2 enters $FV_C$ once and earns no reinvestment interest.
The sale price excludes that coupon. This is a terminal-wealth return that
assumes all coupon cash remains invested until the horizon.

```{python}
#| label: horizon-return-engine
def horizon_report(exit_yield, reinvest_rate, horizon_periods=4):
    """Return a scenario table of terminal wealth and realized return.

    Rates are decimal nominal annual rates; horizon is an integer number of
    half years in [1, 10]. Scalars and arrays broadcast to a common shape.
    Coupons are reinvested at a constant scenario rate, without withdrawals.
    """
    if int(horizon_periods) != horizon_periods or not 1 <= horizon_periods <= maturity_periods:
        raise ValueError("Horizon must be a coupon date through maturity.")
    ey, ri = np.broadcast_arrays(np.atleast_1d(exit_yield), np.atleast_1d(reinvest_rate))
    if not np.all(np.isfinite(ri)) or np.any(ri <= -2):
        raise ValueError("Reinvestment rates must be finite and greater than -200%.")
    coupon_cash = 3*horizon_periods
    powers = np.arange(horizon_periods-1, -1, -1)
    coupon_fv = 3*np.exp(np.log1p(ri[...,None]/2)*powers).sum(axis=-1)
    sale = bond_price(coupon_rate, maturity_periods-horizon_periods, ey)
    wealth = sale+coupon_fv
    return pd.DataFrame({"Exit YTM (%)": 100*ey.ravel(), "Reinvest (%)": 100*ri.ravel(),
        "Sale / principal": sale.ravel(), "Coupon cash": np.full(ey.size,coupon_cash),
        "Reinvestment income": (coupon_fv-coupon_cash).ravel(),
        "Price change": (sale-purchase_price).ravel(), "Terminal wealth": wealth.ravel(),
        "HPR (%)": 100*(wealth/purchase_price-1).ravel(),
        "Annual return (%)": 100*np.expm1(np.log(wealth/purchase_price)/(horizon_periods/2)).ravel()})

base = horizon_report(.05,.05)
show_table(base,5)
```

The unchanged-yield case includes a price decline toward par. Coupon income
more than offsets that decline. A premium bond's coupon rate therefore overstates
its return even when market yields do not change.

## Separate price risk from reinvestment risk

Hold reinvestment at 3% while varying exit yields. Then hold the exit yield at 5%
while varying reinvestment. This separation avoids assuming that sale yields and
all past reinvestment rates move together.

For this two-year horizon, a higher sale yield lowers the value of the remaining
three-year bond. Higher reinvestment rates increase accumulated coupons. These
are opposing effects with different magnitudes and timing.

**Interpretation and decision.** The coupon rate determines contractual cash
payments; it does not measure profit relative to the premium purchase price.
The purchase YTM describes promised cash-flow discounting, while the horizon
return also depends on sale price and reinvestment. The 10% exit scenario shows
that coupon income need not prevent a loss. This five-year bond does not assure
a positive two-year return. A default-free zero-coupon claim maturing at the
client's horizon better matches a fixed nominal liability and eliminates coupon
reinvestment risk. Its purchase price still determines its return; inflation,
transaction costs, and selling before maturity remain relevant.
