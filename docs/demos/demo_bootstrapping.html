The desk question

A fixed-income analyst has Treasury prices. What is today’s value of each future dollar, and how can those values price a bond outside the input set? This demo extends Lecture 4 with a different five-year dataset from payment schedules through curve construction, valuation, forward rates, and validation.

Purpose and assumptions

Sections 1–5 use ten illustrative Treasury-like securities, not market quotes. Face value is $100, settlement is on a coupon date, accrued interest is zero, and payments align every six months. Rates are decimal annual nominal rates compounded semiannually unless stated otherwise. Default, options, taxes, liquidity premiums, and transaction costs are outside this teaching model.

Section 6 separately uses a January 2, 2025 Treasury par-yield snapshot. That historical dataset demonstrates the alternative route of starting with a published par curve. It is neither live data nor a file of individual bond prices.

\[ P_i^{\mathrm{full}}=P_i^{\mathrm{clean}}+AI_i =\sum_j CF_{ij}D(t_j),\qquad D(0)=1. \]

2 Bootstrapping the Discount Factors

For the bond maturing at \(t_n=n/2\), let \(C_n\) be the semiannual coupon in dollars:

\[ D(t_n)=\frac{P_n-C_n\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}D(t_j)}{100+C_n}, \qquad z(t_n)=2\left[D(t_n)^{-1/n}-1\right]. \]

Subtract the value of the earlier coupons, whose discount factors are known. The residual price belongs to the final payment. Dividing by that payment gives its discount factor. The recursion needs one new unknown per instrument.

2.1 The first three steps by hand

\[ D(0.5)=98.10/100=0.981000. \]

\[ D(1)=\frac{99.55-1.75(0.981000)}{101.75}=0.961506143. \]

\[ D(1.5)=\frac{100.10-2[0.981000+0.961506143]}{102}=0.943284193. \]

\[ z(1)=2[D(1)^{-1/2}-1]=3.964208\%. \]

The remaining seven securities use the same recursion through year 5. The tables below retain unrounded factors and show every step.

def bootstrap(full_prices, coupon_payments):
    """Solve an aligned semiannual discount curve from full prices.

    Parameters
    ----------
    full_prices : array-like, shape (n,)
        Prices per 100 face at maturities 0.5, 1.0, ..., n/2 years.
    coupon_payments : array-like, shape (n,)
        Each instrument's coupon dollars per half year, not percentages.

    Returns
    -------
    discounts : ndarray, shape (n,)
        Positive payment-date discount factors.
    trail : pandas.DataFrame
        Earlier coupon PV, residual price, final payment, and solved factor.

    Raises
    ------
    ValueError
        For invalid inputs or a nonpositive implied discount factor.

    Notes
    -----
    Each instrument adds one date. Irregular schedules or missing dates need
    additional modeling and cannot be inserted into this recursion unchanged.
    """
    p = np.asarray(full_prices, dtype=float)
    c = np.asarray(coupon_payments, dtype=float)
    if p.ndim != 1 or p.size == 0 or c.shape != p.shape:
        raise ValueError("Prices and coupons must be matching nonempty vectors.")
    if not np.all(np.isfinite(p)) or not np.all(np.isfinite(c)):
        raise ValueError("Inputs must be finite.")
    if np.any(p <= 0) or np.any(c < 0):
        raise ValueError("Prices must be positive and coupons nonnegative.")
    d = np.empty(len(p))
    rows = []
    for i in range(len(p)):
        earlier_pv = c[i] * d[:i].sum()
        residual = p[i] - earlier_pv
        d[i] = residual / (100 + c[i])
        if d[i] <= 0:
            raise ValueError("The price implies a nonpositive discount factor.")
        rows.append([(i + 1) / 2, earlier_pv, residual, 100 + c[i], d[i]])
    return d, pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=[
        "Years", "Earlier PV", "Residual price", "Final payment", "Discount factor"
    ])

def curve_rates(discounts):
    """Convert aligned half-year factors into spot, par, and forward quotes.

    Parameters
    ----------
    discounts : array-like
        Positive factors at consecutive half-year dates.

    Returns
    -------
    tuple of ndarrays
        Decimal annual semiannual spot rates, par coupons, and annualized
        six-month forwards. The first forward starts today.
    """
    d = np.asarray(discounts, dtype=float)
    if d.ndim != 1 or d.size == 0 or np.any(d <= 0) or not np.all(np.isfinite(d)):
        raise ValueError("Factors must form a finite positive vector.")
    n = np.arange(1, len(d) + 1)
    spots = 2 * np.expm1(-np.log(d) / n)
    par = 2 * (1 - d) / np.cumsum(d)
    forwards = 2 * (np.r_[1.0, d[:-1]] / d - 1)
    return spots, par, forwards

4 Derived Par Coupons and Forward Rates

The par coupon makes a hypothetical bond worth 100. A forward is the break-even rate between adjacent payment dates:

\[ c_{\mathrm{par}}(t_n)=\frac{2[1-D(t_n)]}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}D(t_j)}, \qquad f_{t_{n-1},t_n}^{(2)}=2\left[\frac{D(t_{n-1})}{D(t_n)}-1\right]. \]

The superscript \((2)\) denotes annual nominal rates with semiannual compounding. The six-month interval return is \(f^{(2)}/2\).

5 Interpolation and Model Risk

Suppose a claim pays $100 at 3.25 years. There is no input at that date. Between known dates \(a,b\), define \(w=(t-a)/(b-a)\):

\[ D_{\mathrm{linear}}(t)=(1-w)D(a)+wD(b), \]

\[ D_{\mathrm{log}}(t)=\exp\{(1-w)\log D(a)+w\log D(b)\}. \]

Both methods reproduce the endpoint factors. Log-linear interpolation implies constant continuously compounded forwards within each interval. Neither intermediate value is observed, and neither rule guarantees smooth forwards at the knots.

Show calculation 
def interpolate_discount(payment_times, known_times, known_discounts, method="log"):
    """Interpolate discount factors without unsupported extrapolation.

    Parameters
    ----------
    payment_times : array-like
        Requested maturities in years, within the knot range.
    known_times, known_discounts : array-like
        Increasing dates and matching positive discount factors.
    method : {"log", "linear"}
        Interpolate log factors or levels.

    Returns
    -------
    ndarray
        Factors at the requested dates.

    Raises
    ------
    ValueError
        For invalid knots, factors, method, or requested extrapolation.
    """
    t, x, d = (np.asarray(v, dtype=float) for v in
               (payment_times, known_times, known_discounts))
    if x.ndim != 1 or d.shape != x.shape or x.size < 2:
        raise ValueError("Supply at least two matching knots.")
    if not all(np.all(np.isfinite(v)) for v in (t,x,d)):
        raise ValueError("Dates and factors must be finite.")
    if np.any(np.diff(x) <= 0) or np.any(d <= 0):
        raise ValueError("Dates must increase and factors must be positive.")
    if np.any(t < x[0]) or np.any(t > x[-1]):
        raise ValueError("Extrapolation needs a separate assumption.")
    if method == "log":
        return np.exp(np.interp(t,x,np.log(d)))
    if method == "linear":
        return np.interp(t,x,d)
    raise ValueError("Unknown interpolation method.")

6 Market Data: An Existing Par Curve

These January 2, 2025 observations come from U.S. Treasury Daily Par Yield Curve Rates. They are published curve points, not security-level prices. The embedded snapshot makes the demonstration reproducible without an online data connection.

We interpolate the missing half-year par coupons linearly, then assign a price of 100 to each hypothetical par bond:

\[ P_n=100,\quad C_n=100c_n/2,\quad D(t_n)=\frac{100-C_n\sum_{j<n}D(t_j)}{100+C_n}. \]

This is a teaching reconstruction, not the Treasury’s exact internal curve. The official methodology uses its own market inputs and fitting procedure.

## Bootstrapping the Discount Factors

For the bond maturing at $t_n=n/2$, let $C_n$ be the semiannual coupon in dollars:

$$
D(t_n)=\frac{P_n-C_n\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}D(t_j)}{100+C_n},
\qquad z(t_n)=2\left[D(t_n)^{-1/n}-1\right].
$$

Subtract the value of the earlier coupons, whose discount factors are known.
The residual price belongs to the final payment. Dividing by that payment gives
its discount factor. The recursion needs one new unknown per instrument.

### The first three steps by hand

$$
D(0.5)=98.10/100=0.981000.
$$

$$
D(1)=\frac{99.55-1.75(0.981000)}{101.75}=0.961506143.
$$

$$
D(1.5)=\frac{100.10-2[0.981000+0.961506143]}{102}=0.943284193.
$$

$$
z(1)=2[D(1)^{-1/2}-1]=3.964208\%.
$$

The remaining seven securities use the same recursion through year 5.
The tables below retain unrounded factors and show every step.

```{python}
#| label: bootstrap-functions
def bootstrap(full_prices, coupon_payments):
    """Solve an aligned semiannual discount curve from full prices.

    Parameters
    ----------
    full_prices : array-like, shape (n,)
        Prices per 100 face at maturities 0.5, 1.0, ..., n/2 years.
    coupon_payments : array-like, shape (n,)
        Each instrument's coupon dollars per half year, not percentages.

    Returns
    -------
    discounts : ndarray, shape (n,)
        Positive payment-date discount factors.
    trail : pandas.DataFrame
        Earlier coupon PV, residual price, final payment, and solved factor.

    Raises
    ------
    ValueError
        For invalid inputs or a nonpositive implied discount factor.

    Notes
    -----
    Each instrument adds one date. Irregular schedules or missing dates need
    additional modeling and cannot be inserted into this recursion unchanged.
    """
    p = np.asarray(full_prices, dtype=float)
    c = np.asarray(coupon_payments, dtype=float)
    if p.ndim != 1 or p.size == 0 or c.shape != p.shape:
        raise ValueError("Prices and coupons must be matching nonempty vectors.")
    if not np.all(np.isfinite(p)) or not np.all(np.isfinite(c)):
        raise ValueError("Inputs must be finite.")
    if np.any(p <= 0) or np.any(c < 0):
        raise ValueError("Prices must be positive and coupons nonnegative.")
    d = np.empty(len(p))
    rows = []
    for i in range(len(p)):
        earlier_pv = c[i] * d[:i].sum()
        residual = p[i] - earlier_pv
        d[i] = residual / (100 + c[i])
        if d[i] <= 0:
            raise ValueError("The price implies a nonpositive discount factor.")
        rows.append([(i + 1) / 2, earlier_pv, residual, 100 + c[i], d[i]])
    return d, pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=[
        "Years", "Earlier PV", "Residual price", "Final payment", "Discount factor"
    ])

def curve_rates(discounts):
    """Convert aligned half-year factors into spot, par, and forward quotes.

    Parameters
    ----------
    discounts : array-like
        Positive factors at consecutive half-year dates.

    Returns
    -------
    tuple of ndarrays
        Decimal annual semiannual spot rates, par coupons, and annualized
        six-month forwards. The first forward starts today.
    """
    d = np.asarray(discounts, dtype=float)
    if d.ndim != 1 or d.size == 0 or np.any(d <= 0) or not np.all(np.isfinite(d)):
        raise ValueError("Factors must form a finite positive vector.")
    n = np.arange(1, len(d) + 1)
    spots = 2 * np.expm1(-np.log(d) / n)
    par = 2 * (1 - d) / np.cumsum(d)
    forwards = 2 * (np.r_[1.0, d[:-1]] / d - 1)
    return spots, par, forwards
```

## Derived Par Coupons and Forward Rates

The par coupon makes a hypothetical bond worth 100. A forward is the break-even
rate between adjacent payment dates:

$$
c_{\mathrm{par}}(t_n)=\frac{2[1-D(t_n)]}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}D(t_j)},
\qquad
f_{t_{n-1},t_n}^{(2)}=2\left[\frac{D(t_{n-1})}{D(t_n)}-1\right].
$$

The superscript $(2)$ denotes annual nominal rates with semiannual compounding.
The six-month interval return is $f^{(2)}/2$.

## Interpolation and Model Risk

Suppose a claim pays \$100 at 3.25 years. There is no input at that date.
Between known dates $a,b$, define $w=(t-a)/(b-a)$:

$$
D_{\mathrm{linear}}(t)=(1-w)D(a)+wD(b),
$$

$$
D_{\mathrm{log}}(t)=\exp\{(1-w)\log D(a)+w\log D(b)\}.
$$

Both methods reproduce the endpoint factors. Log-linear interpolation implies
constant continuously compounded forwards within each interval. Neither
intermediate value is observed, and neither rule guarantees smooth forwards
at the knots.

```{python}
#| label: bootstrap-interpolation
def interpolate_discount(payment_times, known_times, known_discounts, method="log"):
    """Interpolate discount factors without unsupported extrapolation.

    Parameters
    ----------
    payment_times : array-like
        Requested maturities in years, within the knot range.
    known_times, known_discounts : array-like
        Increasing dates and matching positive discount factors.
    method : {"log", "linear"}
        Interpolate log factors or levels.

    Returns
    -------
    ndarray
        Factors at the requested dates.

    Raises
    ------
    ValueError
        For invalid knots, factors, method, or requested extrapolation.
    """
    t, x, d = (np.asarray(v, dtype=float) for v in
               (payment_times, known_times, known_discounts))
    if x.ndim != 1 or d.shape != x.shape or x.size < 2:
        raise ValueError("Supply at least two matching knots.")
    if not all(np.all(np.isfinite(v)) for v in (t,x,d)):
        raise ValueError("Dates and factors must be finite.")
    if np.any(np.diff(x) <= 0) or np.any(d <= 0):
        raise ValueError("Dates must increase and factors must be positive.")
    if np.any(t < x[0]) or np.any(t > x[-1]):
        raise ValueError("Extrapolation needs a separate assumption.")
    if method == "log":
        return np.exp(np.interp(t,x,np.log(d)))
    if method == "linear":
        return np.interp(t,x,d)
    raise ValueError("Unknown interpolation method.")

knots, knot_discounts = np.r_[0.,times], np.r_[1.,discounts]
offgrid_log = interpolate_discount([3.25],knots,knot_discounts)[0]
offgrid_linear = interpolate_discount([3.25],knots,knot_discounts,"linear")[0]
show_table(pd.DataFrame({
    "Method": ["Log-linear","Linear discounts"],
    "D(3.25)": [offgrid_log,offgrid_linear],
    "PV of 100": [100*offgrid_log,100*offgrid_linear],
}),8)
grid = np.linspace(3,3.5,101)
difference = 100*(interpolate_discount(grid,knots,knot_discounts,"linear") -
                  interpolate_discount(grid,knots,knot_discounts))
fig,ax = plt.subplots()
ax.plot(grid,difference)
ax.axhline(0,color="#777",linewidth=1)
ax.set(xlabel="Payment date (years)",ylabel="Linear minus log-linear PV per $100",
       title="Same endpoint prices, different intermediate values")
fig.tight_layout()
plt.show()
```

The difference is small over this short interval but not zero. Larger gaps and
larger positions can make interpolation more important. An exact fit at known
dates cannot establish the correct shape between them.

## Market Data: An Existing Par Curve

These **January 2, 2025** observations come from
[U.S. Treasury Daily Par Yield Curve Rates](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?field_tdr_date_value=2025&type=daily_treasury_yield_curve).
They are published curve points, not security-level prices. The embedded snapshot
makes the demonstration reproducible without an online data connection.

We interpolate the missing half-year par coupons linearly, then assign a price
of 100 to each hypothetical par bond:

$$
P_n=100,\quad C_n=100c_n/2,\quad
D(t_n)=\frac{100-C_n\sum_{j<n}D(t_j)}{100+C_n}.
$$

This is a **teaching reconstruction**, not the Treasury's exact internal curve.
The [official methodology](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/financing-the-government/interest-rate-statistics/treasury-yield-curve-methodology)
uses its own market inputs and fitting procedure.
