The desk question

A manager holds three cash bonds and asks: if yields rise by 50 basis points, how much will we lose, and can a duration estimate be trusted? Start with the payments you own, then measure risk.

  1. Read the position file: distinguish face value from market value.
  2. Discount each payment: recover the price and the timing of its present value.
  3. Measure sensitivity: translate duration into dollars per basis point.
  4. Shock and reprice: compare a tangent-line estimate with actual discounted cash flows.
  5. Report the exception: explain why a curve twist needs more than one risk number.

Purpose and Assumptions

This demo prices a small bond portfolio from contractual cash flows, measures Macaulay duration, modified duration, DV01/PVBP, and convexity, and compares approximations with exact repricing. All bonds and yields are illustrative, market-like inputs, not live quotes. Bonds pay semiannual coupons; yields are nominal annual yields compounded semiannually; settlement is on a coupon date; default, taxes, accrued interest, and embedded options are ignored.

1 Full Cash-Flow Pricing and Risk Measures

\[ \begin{aligned} F&=\text{face value in dollars}, & c&=\text{annual coupon rate},\\ T&=\text{maturity in years}, & y&=\text{nominal annual yield},\\ m&=2\text{ payments per year}.&& \end{aligned} \]

\[ n=mT,\qquad t_k=\frac{k}{m},\qquad CF_k=\frac{Fc}{m}+F\,\mathbf{1}_{\{k=n\}},\qquad k=1,\ldots,n. \]

\[ PV_k=\frac{CF_k}{(1+y/m)^k},\qquad P=\sum_{k=1}^{n}PV_k. \]

\[ D_{\mathrm{Mac}}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_kPV_k}{P}, \qquad D_{\mathrm{mod}}=\frac{D_{\mathrm{Mac}}}{1+y/m} =-\frac{1}{P}\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}. \]

\[ DV01=PVBP\approx P D_{\mathrm{mod}}\times10^{-4} \quad\text{(dollars per basis point)}. \]

\[ C=\frac{1}{P}\frac{\partial^2P}{\partial y^2} =\frac{1}{P}\sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{k(k+1)CF_k}{m^2(1+y/m)^{k+2}}. \]

def bond_metrics(face, coupon_rate, maturity, ytm, frequency=2):
    """Price a fixed-rate bullet bond and calculate annual-yield sensitivities.

    Parameters
    ----------
    face : float
        Face value in dollars, including the entire position if appropriate.
    coupon_rate : float
        Annual coupon rate as a decimal (0.0575 means 5.75%).
    maturity : float
        Remaining years, containing an integer number of coupon periods.
    ytm : float
        Nominal annual decimal yield, compounded frequency times per year.
    frequency : int, default 2
        Equally spaced coupon payments per year.

    Returns
    -------
    tuple[float, float, float, float, float]
        Full dollar price, Macaulay duration in years, modified duration,
        positive first-order DV01 in dollars per bp, and annual convexity.
        Convexity excludes the Taylor approximation's one-half factor.

    Raises
    ------
    ValueError
        For nonfinite inputs, nonpositive face/maturity, negative coupons,
        invalid frequency/period count, or a nonpositive discount factor.

    Notes
    -----
    Settlement is on a coupon date, so accrued interest is zero. Principal is
    repaid at maturity. Default and embedded options are excluded.
    DV01 is derivative-based, rather than an exact finite 1 bp price change.
    """
    inputs = [face, coupon_rate, maturity, ytm, frequency]
    if not np.isfinite(inputs).all() or face <= 0 or maturity <= 0 or coupon_rate < 0:
        raise ValueError("Use finite inputs, positive face/maturity, and nonnegative coupons.")
    if frequency <= 0 or int(frequency) != frequency or 1 + ytm / frequency <= 0:
        raise ValueError("Use a positive integer frequency and a valid discount factor.")
    periods = int(round(maturity * frequency))
    if periods < 1 or not np.isclose(periods, maturity * frequency):
        raise ValueError("Maturity must contain an integer number of coupon periods.")
    # Coupon-date settlement places payments at whole coupon periods.
    times = np.arange(1, periods + 1) / frequency
    cash_flows = np.full(periods, face * coupon_rate / frequency)
    cash_flows[-1] += face  # Repay principal with the final coupon.
    period_yield = ytm / frequency
    pv = cash_flows / (1 + period_yield) ** np.arange(1, periods + 1)
    price = pv.sum()
    macaulay = np.sum(times * pv) / price
    modified = macaulay / (1 + period_yield)
    k = np.arange(1, periods + 1)
    # 1/m^2 converts periodic-yield curvature into annual-yield curvature.
    convexity = np.sum(
        cash_flows * k * (k + 1)
        / (frequency**2 * (1 + period_yield) ** (k + 2))
    ) / price
    dv01 = price * modified * 0.0001  # One yield bp equals 0.0001.
    return price, macaulay, modified, dv01, convexity

2 Exact Repricing Versus Approximations

NoteFollow one payment

The corporate bond pays $230,000 every six months: $8,000,000 × 5.75% / 2. Its first payment is worth $230,000 / 1.0305 today, while its final payment is discounted twenty times. Later payments are more sensitive because a yield change affects more discounting periods. This is why maturity alone does not describe the portfolio’s interest-rate risk.

3 Portfolio Aggregation

Each column below is a position; the last column reconciles the whole portfolio. Market values and DV01s sum. Durations and convexities are market-value-weighted averages. Contribution rows show the pieces of those averages.

\[ V_0=\sum_iP_i,\qquad w_i=\frac{P_i}{V_0},\qquad DV01_{\mathrm{port}}=\sum_iDV01_i. \]

\[ D_{\mathrm{port}}=\sum_iw_iD_i =\frac{DV01_{\mathrm{port}}}{V_0\,10^{-4}},\qquad C_{\mathrm{port}}=\sum_iw_iC_i. \]

4 Parallel and Nonparallel Scenarios

A single duration number is designed for a common yield shift. Here we approximate a curve twist with different yield-to-maturity shocks for the three bonds. Repricing is exact for those assigned bond yields, not for a fully specified spot-curve twist. A production key-rate analysis would shock the underlying discount curve and revalue every dated cash flow.

For bond \(i\), let \(b_i\) be its assigned shock in basis points, so \(\Delta y_i=b_i/10{,}000\). Each method starts from the same dollar market value \(P_i\), not face value. Here \(D_i\) is modified duration and \(C_i\) is annualized convexity, both evaluated at the bond’s initial yield.

\[ L_i^{D}=-P_iD_i\Delta y_i=-DV01_i\,b_i, \qquad A_i^C=\frac12P_iC_i(\Delta y_i)^2. \]

\[ L_i^{D+C}=L_i^D+A_i^C,\qquad L_i^{\mathrm{exact}}= \sum_{k=1}^{n_i}\frac{CF_{i,k}}{[1+(y_i+\Delta y_i)/m]^k}-P_i. \]

\[ L_{\mathrm{port}}^D=\sum_iL_i^D,\qquad L_{\mathrm{port}}^{D+C}=\sum_iL_i^{D+C},\qquad L_{\mathrm{port}}^{\mathrm{exact}}=\sum_iL_i^{\mathrm{exact}}. \]

Under the parallel +50 bp shock, every \(\Delta y_i=0.005\), so the same results can also be obtained directly from the portfolio measures:

\[ L_{\mathrm{port}}^D=-50\,DV01_{\mathrm{port}}, \qquad L_{\mathrm{port}}^{D+C} =-V_0D_{\mathrm{port}}(0.005)+\frac12V_0C_{\mathrm{port}}(0.005)^2. \]

For the twist scenarios, use each bond’s own shock before summing. There is no common \(\Delta y\) to insert into the portfolio-duration formula.

scenario_shocks = {
    "Parallel +50 bp": np.array([50, 50, 50]),
    "Bear steepener": np.array([10, 35, 75]),
    "Bull flattener": np.array([-15, -45, -90]),
}

def scenario_contributions(risk, shocks_bp, frequency=2):
    """Calculate position-level dollar P&L under an assigned yield scenario.

    Parameters
    ----------
    risk : pandas.DataFrame
        One row per bond with the inputs and full-precision bond_metrics outputs.
    shocks_bp : array-like
        One nominal annual yield shock per row, in basis points and row order.
    frequency : int, default 2
        Coupon frequency shared by these illustrative bonds.

    Returns
    -------
    pandas.DataFrame
        Per-position shock, first-order P&L, convexity addition, combined P&L,
        and exact shocked value minus initial value, all P&L fields in dollars.

    Raises
    ------
    ValueError
        If there is not exactly one finite yield shock per bond.

    Notes
    -----
    This applies bond-specific YTM shocks, not a full spot-curve revaluation.
    Original duration and convexity remain fixed for the Taylor approximation.
    """
    bp = np.asarray(shocks_bp, dtype=float)
    if bp.shape != (len(risk),) or not np.isfinite(bp).all():
        raise ValueError("Provide one finite shock per bond in portfolio order.")
    dy = bp / 10000
    values = risk["Price ($)"].to_numpy()
    first_order = -risk["DV01/PVBP ($/bp)"].to_numpy() * bp
    curvature = 0.5 * values * risk["Convexity"].to_numpy() * dy**2
    # Reprice cash flows independently; do not reuse a Taylor-estimated value.
    shocked_values = np.array([
        bond_metrics(row["Face value ($mm)"] * 1e6, row["Coupon rate"],
                     row["Maturity (years)"], row["Yield"] + shock, frequency)[0]
        for (_, row), shock in zip(risk.iterrows(), dy)
    ])
    return pd.DataFrame({
        "Yield shock (bp)": bp,
        "Duration P&L ($)": first_order,
        "Convexity addition ($)": curvature,
        "Duration + convexity P&L ($)": first_order + curvature,
        "Exact P&L ($)": shocked_values - values,
    }, index=risk["Bond"].to_numpy())

5 Validation Checks

Validation asks two different questions: do the calculations reconcile? and does the risk allocation make economic sense?

The chart compares the share of money invested with the share of sensitivity to a common 1 bp yield increase:

\[ \text{Capital share}_i=100\frac{P_i}{V_0},\qquad \text{DV01 share}_i=100\frac{DV01_i}{DV01_{\mathrm{port}}}. \]

Each set of shares must sum to 100%. If a bond’s DV01 bar is longer than its market-value bar, it contributes more risk per dollar invested than the portfolio average. The long corporate bond can therefore dominate rate risk without being the largest investment. This chart diagnoses concentration; it does not by itself validate the pricing formula or measure credit risk.

## The desk question {.unnumbered}

A manager holds three cash bonds and asks: **if yields rise by 50 basis points,
how much will we lose, and can a duration estimate be trusted?** Start with the
payments you own, then measure risk.

1. **Read the position file:** distinguish face value from market value.
2. **Discount each payment:** recover the price and the timing of its present value.
3. **Measure sensitivity:** translate duration into dollars per basis point.
4. **Shock and reprice:** compare a tangent-line estimate with actual discounted cash flows.
5. **Report the exception:** explain why a curve twist needs more than one risk number.

```{python}
def readable_table(frame):
    """Format a teaching table without changing its underlying numerical values.

    Parameters
    ----------
    frame : pandas.DataFrame
        Display data. attrs["whole_table"] requests a single reconciliation
        table; attrs["decimals"] chooses the number of displayed decimals.

    str
        HTML markup suitable for both Quarto HTML and PDF conversion.

    Notes
    -----
    Wide, short tables are transposed. Other wide tables are split into panels.
    Formatting applies only at display time, preserving full-precision results.
    """
    whole_table = frame.attrs.get("whole_table", False)
    decimals = frame.attrs.get("decimals", 4)
    frame = frame.copy()
    if not whole_table and len(frame.columns) and frame.iloc[:, 0].dtype == object:
        frame = frame.set_index(frame.columns[0])
    if not whole_table and len(frame.columns) > 6 and len(frame) <= 4:
        frame = frame.T
    panels = []
    panel_width = len(frame.columns) if whole_table else 3
    for start in range(0, len(frame.columns), panel_width):
        part = frame.iloc[:, start:start+panel_width]
        markup = part.to_html(border=0, na_rep="", float_format=lambda v: f"{v:,.{decimals}f}")

        cols = "<colgroup>" + "".join(f'<col style="width:{w}%">' for w in widths) + "</colgroup>"
        end = markup.index(">") + 1
        panels.append(markup[:end] + cols + markup[end:])
    return "\n".join(panels)
```

## Full Cash-Flow Pricing and Risk Measures

$$
\begin{aligned}
F&=\text{face value in dollars}, & c&=\text{annual coupon rate},\\
T&=\text{maturity in years}, & y&=\text{nominal annual yield},\\
m&=2\text{ payments per year}.&&
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
n=mT,\qquad t_k=\frac{k}{m},\qquad
CF_k=\frac{Fc}{m}+F\,\mathbf{1}_{\{k=n\}},\qquad k=1,\ldots,n.
$$

$$
PV_k=\frac{CF_k}{(1+y/m)^k},\qquad P=\sum_{k=1}^{n}PV_k.
$$

$$
D_{\mathrm{Mac}}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_kPV_k}{P},
\qquad D_{\mathrm{mod}}=\frac{D_{\mathrm{Mac}}}{1+y/m}
=-\frac{1}{P}\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}.
$$

$$
DV01=PVBP\approx P D_{\mathrm{mod}}\times10^{-4}
\quad\text{(dollars per basis point)}.
$$

$$
C=\frac{1}{P}\frac{\partial^2P}{\partial y^2}
=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{k=1}^{n}
\frac{k(k+1)CF_k}{m^2(1+y/m)^{k+2}}.
$$

```{python}
#| label: bond-functions
def bond_metrics(face, coupon_rate, maturity, ytm, frequency=2):
    """Price a fixed-rate bullet bond and calculate annual-yield sensitivities.

    Parameters
    ----------
    face : float
        Face value in dollars, including the entire position if appropriate.
    coupon_rate : float
        Annual coupon rate as a decimal (0.0575 means 5.75%).
    maturity : float
        Remaining years, containing an integer number of coupon periods.
    ytm : float
        Nominal annual decimal yield, compounded frequency times per year.
    frequency : int, default 2
        Equally spaced coupon payments per year.

    Returns
    -------
    tuple[float, float, float, float, float]
        Full dollar price, Macaulay duration in years, modified duration,
        positive first-order DV01 in dollars per bp, and annual convexity.
        Convexity excludes the Taylor approximation's one-half factor.

    Raises
    ------
    ValueError
        For nonfinite inputs, nonpositive face/maturity, negative coupons,
        invalid frequency/period count, or a nonpositive discount factor.

    Notes
    -----
    Settlement is on a coupon date, so accrued interest is zero. Principal is
    repaid at maturity. Default and embedded options are excluded.
    DV01 is derivative-based, rather than an exact finite 1 bp price change.
    """
    inputs = [face, coupon_rate, maturity, ytm, frequency]
    if not np.isfinite(inputs).all() or face <= 0 or maturity <= 0 or coupon_rate < 0:
        raise ValueError("Use finite inputs, positive face/maturity, and nonnegative coupons.")
    if frequency <= 0 or int(frequency) != frequency or 1 + ytm / frequency <= 0:
        raise ValueError("Use a positive integer frequency and a valid discount factor.")
    periods = int(round(maturity * frequency))
    if periods < 1 or not np.isclose(periods, maturity * frequency):
        raise ValueError("Maturity must contain an integer number of coupon periods.")
    # Coupon-date settlement places payments at whole coupon periods.
    times = np.arange(1, periods + 1) / frequency
    cash_flows = np.full(periods, face * coupon_rate / frequency)
    cash_flows[-1] += face  # Repay principal with the final coupon.
    period_yield = ytm / frequency
    pv = cash_flows / (1 + period_yield) ** np.arange(1, periods + 1)
    price = pv.sum()
    macaulay = np.sum(times * pv) / price
    modified = macaulay / (1 + period_yield)
    k = np.arange(1, periods + 1)
    # 1/m^2 converts periodic-yield curvature into annual-yield curvature.
    convexity = np.sum(
        cash_flows * k * (k + 1)
        / (frequency**2 * (1 + period_yield) ** (k + 2))
    ) / price
    dv01 = price * modified * 0.0001  # One yield bp equals 0.0001.
    return price, macaulay, modified, dv01, convexity

rows = []
for bond in bonds.itertuples(index=False):
    face = bond[1] * 1_000_000
    metrics = bond_metrics(face, bond[2], bond[3], bond[4], frequency)

metrics = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["Price ($)", "Macaulay duration",
                                      "Modified duration", "DV01/PVBP ($/bp)", "Convexity"])
```

## Portfolio Aggregation

Each column below is a position; the last column reconciles the whole portfolio.
Market values and DV01s **sum**. Durations and convexities are **market-value-weighted
averages**. Contribution rows show the pieces of those averages.

$$
V_0=\sum_iP_i,\qquad w_i=\frac{P_i}{V_0},\qquad
DV01_{\mathrm{port}}=\sum_iDV01_i.
$$

$$
D_{\mathrm{port}}=\sum_iw_iD_i
=\frac{DV01_{\mathrm{port}}}{V_0\,10^{-4}},\qquad
C_{\mathrm{port}}=\sum_iw_iC_i.
$$

## Parallel and Nonparallel Scenarios

A single duration number is designed for a common yield shift. Here we approximate
a curve twist with different yield-to-maturity shocks for the three bonds.
Repricing is exact for those assigned bond yields, not for a fully specified
spot-curve twist. A production key-rate analysis would shock the underlying
discount curve and revalue every dated cash flow.

For bond $i$, let $b_i$ be its assigned shock in basis points, so
$\Delta y_i=b_i/10{,}000$. Each method starts from the **same dollar market value**
$P_i$, not face value.
Here $D_i$ is modified duration and $C_i$ is annualized convexity, both
evaluated at the bond's initial yield.

$$
L_i^{D}=-P_iD_i\Delta y_i=-DV01_i\,b_i,
\qquad A_i^C=\frac12P_iC_i(\Delta y_i)^2.
$$

$$
L_i^{D+C}=L_i^D+A_i^C,\qquad
L_i^{\mathrm{exact}}=
\sum_{k=1}^{n_i}\frac{CF_{i,k}}{[1+(y_i+\Delta y_i)/m]^k}-P_i.
$$

$$
L_{\mathrm{port}}^D=\sum_iL_i^D,\qquad
L_{\mathrm{port}}^{D+C}=\sum_iL_i^{D+C},\qquad
L_{\mathrm{port}}^{\mathrm{exact}}=\sum_iL_i^{\mathrm{exact}}.
$$

Under the parallel +50 bp shock, every $\Delta y_i=0.005$, so the same results
can also be obtained directly from the portfolio measures:

$$
L_{\mathrm{port}}^D=-50\,DV01_{\mathrm{port}},
\qquad
L_{\mathrm{port}}^{D+C}
=-V_0D_{\mathrm{port}}(0.005)+\frac12V_0C_{\mathrm{port}}(0.005)^2.
$$

For the twist scenarios, use each bond's own shock **before summing**. There is
no common $\Delta y$ to insert into the portfolio-duration formula.

```{python}
"""Assign bond-level yield shocks and retain every contribution to portfolio P&L."""
scenario_shocks = {

    "Bear steepener": np.array([10, 35, 75]),
    "Bull flattener": np.array([-15, -45, -90]),

def scenario_contributions(risk, shocks_bp, frequency=2):
    """Calculate position-level dollar P&L under an assigned yield scenario.

    Parameters
    ----------
    risk : pandas.DataFrame
        One row per bond with the inputs and full-precision bond_metrics outputs.
    shocks_bp : array-like
        One nominal annual yield shock per row, in basis points and row order.
    frequency : int, default 2
        Coupon frequency shared by these illustrative bonds.

    Returns
    -------
    pandas.DataFrame
        Per-position shock, first-order P&L, convexity addition, combined P&L,
        and exact shocked value minus initial value, all P&L fields in dollars.

    Raises
    ------
    ValueError
        If there is not exactly one finite yield shock per bond.

    Notes
    -----
    This applies bond-specific YTM shocks, not a full spot-curve revaluation.
    Original duration and convexity remain fixed for the Taylor approximation.
    """
    bp = np.asarray(shocks_bp, dtype=float)
    if bp.shape != (len(risk),) or not np.isfinite(bp).all():
        raise ValueError("Provide one finite shock per bond in portfolio order.")
    dy = bp / 10000
    values = risk["Price ($)"].to_numpy()
    first_order = -risk["DV01/PVBP ($/bp)"].to_numpy() * bp
    curvature = 0.5 * values * risk["Convexity"].to_numpy() * dy**2
    # Reprice cash flows independently; do not reuse a Taylor-estimated value.
    shocked_values = np.array([
        bond_metrics(row["Face value ($mm)"] * 1e6, row["Coupon rate"],
                     row["Maturity (years)"], row["Yield"] + shock, frequency)[0]
        for (_, row), shock in zip(risk.iterrows(), dy)
    ])
    return pd.DataFrame({
        "Yield shock (bp)": bp,
        "Duration P&L ($)": first_order,
        "Convexity addition ($)": curvature,
        "Duration + convexity P&L ($)": first_order + curvature,
        "Exact P&L ($)": shocked_values - values,
    }, index=risk["Bond"].to_numpy())
```

## Validation Checks

Validation asks two different questions: **do the calculations reconcile?** and
**does the risk allocation make economic sense?**

The chart compares the share of money invested with the share of sensitivity
to a common 1 bp yield increase:

$$
\text{Capital share}_i=100\frac{P_i}{V_0},\qquad
\text{DV01 share}_i=100\frac{DV01_i}{DV01_{\mathrm{port}}}.
$$

Each set of shares must sum to 100%. If a bond's DV01 bar is longer than its
market-value bar, it contributes more risk per dollar invested than the portfolio
average. The long corporate bond can therefore dominate rate risk without being
the largest investment. This chart diagnoses concentration; it does not by
itself validate the pricing formula or measure credit risk.

## Financial Interpretation

Duration is a local slope, convexity is a curvature correction, and exact cash-flow repricing is the benchmark. DV01 translates percentage sensitivity into dollars. Portfolio aggregation is reliable for a stated parallel shock, but nonparallel scenarios require maturity-specific or key-rate exposures.
