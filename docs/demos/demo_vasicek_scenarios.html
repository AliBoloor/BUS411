Purpose and Assumptions

The Vasicek model is

\[ dr_t=\kappa(\theta-r_t)dt+\sigma dW_t. \]

This demo estimates its parameters from a dated monthly U.S. effective federal funds-rate history, simulates scenarios, checks long-run moments, and uses the fitted distribution for a one-year risk decision. The embedded observations are monthly averages for January 2023 through June 2025, recorded in percent and converted to decimals. Source: Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, Effective Federal Funds Rate (FEDFUNDS). Parameters are constant; estimation error, regime shifts, jumps, and term premia are ignored. This short sample is pedagogical and should not be treated as a robust trading calibration.

1 Step 1: Record the observed rate history

The treasury team’s question: how uncertain is next year’s floating interest expense? We will first fit historical persistence, then simulate monthly resets, then turn those resets into dollars. A good fit to history is not evidence that the next policy regime will behave the same way.

The exact monthly transition can be written as an AR(1):

\[ r_{t+\Delta}=\alpha+\phi r_t+\varepsilon_{t+\Delta},\qquad \phi=e^{-\kappa\Delta},\qquad \alpha=\theta(1-\phi). \]

After OLS estimation,

\[ \widehat\kappa=-\frac{\log\widehat\phi}{\Delta},\qquad \widehat\theta=\frac{\widehat\alpha}{1-\widehat\phi},\qquad \widehat\sigma=s_\varepsilon \sqrt{\frac{2\widehat\kappa}{1-\widehat\phi^2}}. \]

Here \(\Delta=1/12\) year, \(\kappa\) is in \(1/\text{year}\), and \(\sigma\) is in decimal rate units per \(\sqrt{\text{year}}\).

Show calculation 
dt = 1 / 12
x = history.iloc[:-1].to_numpy()
y = history.iloc[1:].to_numpy()
design = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(x), x])
alpha_hat, phi_hat = np.linalg.lstsq(design, y, rcond=None)[0]
residuals = y - (alpha_hat + phi_hat * x)
residual_std = residuals.std(ddof=2)

if not 0 < phi_hat < 1:
    raise ValueError("This sample does not imply a stationary Vasicek fit.")

kappa = -np.log(phi_hat) / dt
theta = alpha_hat / (1 - phi_hat)
sigma = residual_std * np.sqrt(2 * kappa / (1 - phi_hat**2))
r0 = float(history.iloc[-1])

half_life = np.log(2) / kappa
long_run_std = sigma / np.sqrt(2 * kappa)

    "Estimated monthly persistence phi": phi_hat,
    "Mean-reversion speed kappa (1/year)": kappa,
    "Long-run mean theta": theta,
    "Volatility sigma (rate/sqrt(year))": sigma,
    "Mean-reversion half-life (years)": half_life,
    "Long-run standard deviation": long_run_std,
}).round(5)

The exact Vasicek transition is normal:

\[ r_{t+\Delta}=\theta+(r_t-\theta)e^{-\kappa\Delta} +\sigma\sqrt{\frac{1-e^{-2\kappa\Delta}}{2\kappa}}Z. \]

Show calculation 
rng = np.random.default_rng(411)

def simulate_vasicek(initial_rate, years, paths, rng):
    steps = int(round(years / dt))
    output = np.empty((steps + 1, paths))
    output[0] = initial_rate
    persistence = np.exp(-kappa * dt)
    innovation_std = sigma * np.sqrt((1 - np.exp(-2 * kappa * dt)) / (2 * kappa))
    for t in range(steps):
        output[t + 1] = (
            theta + (output[t] - theta) * persistence
            + innovation_std * rng.standard_normal(paths)
        )
    return output

paths = simulate_vasicek(r0, years=5, paths=5_000, rng=rng)
time = np.arange(paths.shape[0]) * dt

Vasicek rates are normally distributed, so negative rates are possible. That can be useful in markets where negative policy rates are plausible, but it may be undesirable for other applications. A positivity-preserving model such as CIR changes this feature, at the cost of a different distribution and dynamics.

6 Model Scenario Versus a Historical Shock

6.1 Translate the path into an interest-expense budget

The year-end rate is a reset-rate scenario, not the year’s total interest expense. A $10 million borrowing with monthly resets pays interest throughout the year. For a constant 100 bp loan margin, the simplified annual cash cost is

\[\text{Interest}=10{,}000{,}000\sum_{k=0}^{11}(r_{k/12}+0.01)/12.\]

We use the rate at the start of each monthly period, an equal-month accrual convention, and no compounding of unpaid interest. The loan is illustrative; the historical FEDFUNDS sample supplies the fitted dynamics.

Show calculation 
funding_paths = simulate_vasicek(r0, years=1, paths=50_000,
                               rng=np.random.default_rng(415))
loan_notional, loan_margin = 10_000_000, 0.01
interest_cost = loan_notional * np.sum((funding_paths[:-1] + loan_margin) * dt, axis=0)

7 Validate Long-Run Moments and Units

The stationary Vasicek distribution has

\[ E[r]=\theta, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(r)=\frac{\sigma^2}{2\kappa}. \]

Show calculation

                              rng=np.random.default_rng(413))[-1]
simulated_mean = long_paths.mean()
simulated_std = long_paths.std(ddof=1)

assert kappa > 0 and sigma > 0

8 Financial Interpretation

Mean reversion makes the expected rate move toward \(\theta\), while volatility keeps future rates dispersed. \(\kappa\) determines how quickly today’s rate loses influence; \(\sigma\) determines scenario width. The model is a disciplined scenario generator, not a forecast guarantee. Calibration window, probability measure, structural breaks, and the normal model’s negative-rate tail all affect how its output should be used.

## Step 2: Estimate Vasicek parameters

```{python}
#| label: fig-observed-history
#| fig-cap: "Monthly effective federal funds rates, January 2023–June 2025; the calibration sample is short and policy-driven."
#| fig-alt: "Historical monthly rates rise toward 5.33 percent, plateau, and decline to 4.33 percent."

ax.plot(history.index, 100 * history, "o-", color="#214b3e", markersize=4)
ax.set(ylabel="Monthly average rate (%)", xlabel="Observation month")
fig.autofmt_xdate(rotation=30)
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
```

The exact monthly transition can be written as an AR(1):

$$
r_{t+\Delta}=\alpha+\phi r_t+\varepsilon_{t+\Delta},\qquad
\phi=e^{-\kappa\Delta},\qquad \alpha=\theta(1-\phi).
$$

After OLS estimation,

$$
\widehat\kappa=-\frac{\log\widehat\phi}{\Delta},\qquad
\widehat\theta=\frac{\widehat\alpha}{1-\widehat\phi},\qquad
\widehat\sigma=s_\varepsilon
\sqrt{\frac{2\widehat\kappa}{1-\widehat\phi^2}}.
$$

Here $\Delta=1/12$ year, $\kappa$ is in $1/\text{year}$, and $\sigma$ is in
decimal rate units per $\sqrt{\text{year}}$.

```{python}
#| label: parameters
dt = 1 / 12
x = history.iloc[:-1].to_numpy()
y = history.iloc[1:].to_numpy()
design = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(x), x])
alpha_hat, phi_hat = np.linalg.lstsq(design, y, rcond=None)[0]
residuals = y - (alpha_hat + phi_hat * x)
residual_std = residuals.std(ddof=2)

if not 0 < phi_hat < 1:
    raise ValueError("This sample does not imply a stationary Vasicek fit.")

kappa = -np.log(phi_hat) / dt
theta = alpha_hat / (1 - phi_hat)
sigma = residual_std * np.sqrt(2 * kappa / (1 - phi_hat**2))
r0 = float(history.iloc[-1])

half_life = np.log(2) / kappa
long_run_std = sigma / np.sqrt(2 * kappa)

pd.Series({
    "Current rate": r0,
    "Estimated monthly persistence phi": phi_hat,
    "Mean-reversion speed kappa (1/year)": kappa,
    "Long-run mean theta": theta,
    "Volatility sigma (rate/sqrt(year))": sigma,
    "Mean-reversion half-life (years)": half_life,
    "Long-run standard deviation": long_run_std,
}).round(5)
```

- $\kappa$ controls how quickly shocks decay; the half-life is $\ln(2)/\kappa$.
- $\theta$ is the long-run mean under the chosen probability measure.
- $\sigma$ controls the size of random innovations and the long-run dispersion.

The exact Vasicek transition is normal:

$$
r_{t+\Delta}=\theta+(r_t-\theta)e^{-\kappa\Delta}
+\sigma\sqrt{\frac{1-e^{-2\kappa\Delta}}{2\kappa}}Z.
$$

```{python}
#| label: simulation
rng = np.random.default_rng(411)

def simulate_vasicek(initial_rate, years, paths, rng):
    steps = int(round(years / dt))
    output = np.empty((steps + 1, paths))
    output[0] = initial_rate
    persistence = np.exp(-kappa * dt)
    innovation_std = sigma * np.sqrt((1 - np.exp(-2 * kappa * dt)) / (2 * kappa))
    for t in range(steps):
        output[t + 1] = (
            theta + (output[t] - theta) * persistence
            + innovation_std * rng.standard_normal(paths)
        )
    return output

paths = simulate_vasicek(r0, years=5, paths=5_000, rng=rng)
time = np.arange(paths.shape[0]) * dt
```

Vasicek rates are normally distributed, so negative rates are possible. That can be useful in markets where negative policy rates are plausible, but it may be undesirable for other applications. A positivity-preserving model such as CIR changes this feature, at the cost of a different distribution and dynamics.

## Model Scenario Versus a Historical Shock

### Translate the path into an interest-expense budget

The year-end rate is a **reset-rate** scenario, not the year's total interest
expense. A $10 million borrowing with monthly resets pays interest throughout
the year. For a constant 100 bp loan margin, the simplified annual cash cost is

$$\text{Interest}=10{,}000{,}000\sum_{k=0}^{11}(r_{k/12}+0.01)/12.$$

We use the rate at the start of each monthly period, an equal-month accrual
convention, and no compounding of unpaid interest. The loan is illustrative;
the historical FEDFUNDS sample supplies the fitted dynamics.

```{python}
#| label: funding-cash-budget
funding_paths = simulate_vasicek(r0, years=1, paths=50_000,
                               rng=np.random.default_rng(415))

flat_rate_budget = loan_notional * (r0 + loan_margin)
funding_budget = pd.Series({
    "Flat current-rate budget ($)": flat_rate_budget,

    "95th-percentile expense ($)": np.quantile(interest_cost, 0.95),
```

## Validate Long-Run Moments and Units

The stationary Vasicek distribution has

$$
E[r]=\theta,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(r)=\frac{\sigma^2}{2\kappa}.
$$

```{python}
#| label: validation
long_paths = simulate_vasicek(r0, years=20, paths=30_000,
                              rng=np.random.default_rng(413))[-1]
simulated_mean = long_paths.mean()
simulated_std = long_paths.std(ddof=1)

assert kappa > 0 and sigma > 0
assert abs(simulated_mean - theta) < 0.0005
assert abs(simulated_std - long_run_std) < 0.0005
assert abs((0.0001 * 10_000) - 1.0) < 1e-12

pd.Series({
    "Theoretical long-run mean": round(theta, 5),
    "Simulated 20-year mean": round(simulated_mean, 5),
    "Theoretical long-run standard deviation": round(long_run_std, 5),
    "Simulated 20-year standard deviation": round(simulated_std, 5),
    "Decimal 0.0001 equals one basis point": True,
})
```

## Financial Interpretation

Mean reversion makes the expected rate move toward $\theta$, while volatility keeps future rates dispersed. $\kappa$ determines how quickly today's rate loses influence; $\sigma$ determines scenario width. The model is a disciplined scenario generator, not a forecast guarantee. Calibration window, probability measure, structural breaks, and the normal model's negative-rate tail all affect how its output should be used.
